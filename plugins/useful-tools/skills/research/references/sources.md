# Sources and confidence

## Source preference

Prefer primary sources: vendor and project documentation, standards, laws and agency publications, original studies, release notes, source code. Use secondary sources (news, blogs, forums, aggregators) as pointers to the primary source or when none exists, and say so in the note.

Discard and mention in the sources list with a reason: pages that only restate another source, undated SEO content, pages that could not be read, sources with an obvious conflict of interest when a neutral one exists.

## Confidence

| Level | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| high | primary source, current, and confirmed by an independent second source |
| medium | one reputable source, unconfirmed; or a confirmed claim from a source stale for the topic (see adjustments) |
| low | secondary source only, contradicted elsewhere, or source date missing |

Adjustments:

- Independent means different authors who did not copy each other. Two articles citing the same press release are one source.
- Age matters by topic: for versions, prices and APIs, months are old; for history or established science, years are fine. Note the source date either way.
- A missing date caps confidence at low unless the content itself dates the claim.
- A claim read only in a search snippet is not read. Fetch the page or mark it unverified.
- A subagent's claim without a verbatim quote is unverified.

## Contradictions

Record both claims with their sources and confidence in chat. Do not pick a winner unless one source is clearly primary and the other secondary; even then, keep the conflict visible. Contradictions become open questions in the output.

## Quotes

Short and verbatim, in the original language. Add a translation only where the output language differs and the meaning is not obvious.
